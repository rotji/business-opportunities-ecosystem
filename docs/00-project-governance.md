# Project Governance: Preserve Discoveries, Do Not Repeat Work

**Status: Governing principle — applies to the entire project**

This document establishes a mandatory rule for every exploration, research task, analysis, discussion, decision, and future workstream in this repository.

## The principle

> **We preserve our discoveries as we progress, avoid repeating completed investigations, and update earlier conclusions only when new evidence warrants it.**

This is not a suggestion or a preference. It is a governing operating rule for the entire Business Opportunities Ecosystem project.

## 1. Preserve the journey

Every meaningful investigation must leave a durable record in this repository. Preserve:

- the question or problem investigated;
- the scope, market, geography, and customer segment considered;
- companies, platforms, products, people, and organisations identified;
- evidence, source links, and dates;
- findings and what those findings actually establish;
- assumptions, hypotheses, uncertainty, and limitations;
- decisions made, including decisions not to proceed;
- unanswered questions and the next practical action.

Do not allow important discoveries, contact details, research findings, or decisions to exist only in conversation.

## 2. Check before starting: no unnecessary repetition

Before beginning a new investigation or continuing a workstream:

1. Review the repository README, relevant governing documents, research files, and research log.
2. Identify what has already been investigated and the exact point where work stopped.
3. Reuse established findings and source references instead of researching the same ground again.
4. State the specific unresolved question or new objective that justifies the next step.
5. Continue from the last established point.

A topic being mentioned again does not automatically justify restarting its investigation. Do not repeat work merely to produce a fresh summary, a differently worded document, or another list of the same providers.

## 3. When revisiting completed work is permitted

Earlier work may be revisited when there is a clear reason, including:

- new evidence has appeared;
- a source has changed, become unavailable, or been corrected;
- a company, product, regulation, price, partnership programme, or market condition has materially changed;
- a previous conclusion is challenged by credible evidence;
- an earlier assumption needs validation;
- a new question requires a different scope or level of detail.

When revisiting, identify what is new and why the earlier record needs attention. Do not silently overwrite history or present an old discovery as new.

## 4. Update; do not erase the discovery trail

When new evidence changes an earlier conclusion:

1. Locate the original research record.
2. Add the new evidence with its source and date.
3. Explain what changed, what remains valid, and why the interpretation or decision is different.
4. Update the current conclusion and relevant cross-references.
5. Preserve the historical context wherever practical.

The objective is a traceable evolution of understanding—not a collection of disconnected reports or repeatedly rewritten conclusions.

## 5. Separate evidence, interpretation, and decisions

Every research record must distinguish:

- **Evidence:** what a source or direct observation actually says.
- **Finding:** what the evidence supports.
- **Interpretation:** what the finding may mean.
- **Hypothesis:** what remains to be tested.
- **Decision:** what action, if any, has been justified.
- **Unknown:** what has not yet been established.

Record source URLs, dates, geography, and relevant limitations. Do not turn assumptions, marketing claims, or unverified statements into facts through repetition.

## 6. Every work session must establish continuity

At the end of a meaningful work session, update the appropriate project record with:

- work completed;
- discoveries and evidence added;
- files changed;
- decisions and their rationale;
- unresolved questions;
- the precise next step.

At the beginning of the next session, use those records to resume. Do not ask the project to rediscover what it has already established.

## 7. Scope of authority

This principle applies to:

- every opportunity category and industry;
- all research workstreams and subprojects;
- company, platform, product, and competitor investigations;
- customer and market engagement;
- partnership and commercial opportunity research;
- technical, architectural, and product discussions;
- all contributors and future collaborators working in this repository.

It governs how the project is conducted, regardless of the tool, platform, or format used.

## 8. Practical test before doing work

Before starting, ask:

1. Have we already investigated this?
2. Where is the existing record?
3. What exactly is new or unresolved?
4. What evidence or changed scope justifies revisiting it?
5. How will the result be added without losing the earlier discovery trail?

If there is no new question, evidence, or justified scope, continue from the existing work instead of repeating it.

## Governing commitment

**Preserve what we learn. Resume where we stopped. Revisit only with a reason. Update conclusions when evidence changes. Never confuse repeated activity with progress.**

This rule takes precedence over convenience, conversational recaps, and the temptation to restart an investigation from the beginning.
