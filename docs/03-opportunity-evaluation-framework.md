# Opportunity Evaluation Framework

Use this framework before recommending entry into a market or building a product.

## Research questions

1. What problem is being addressed, and who experiences it?
2. Which established providers and emerging startups address it?
3. What do their products actually do?
4. Which customers use them, and what evidence supports adoption?
5. How do providers price, distribute, and earn revenue?
6. What integrations, APIs, partner programmes, reseller routes, and implementation channels exist?
7. What barriers affect adoption in the target market?
8. Is the apparent gap real, important, and underserved?
9. Is there a concrete customer request, purchasing intent, incentive, or other reason to act?
10. Who else may enter this gap, and how will we monitor developments?

## Evidence discipline

For each finding, record:
- Claim or observation
- Source and URL
- Publication or access date
- Geography and customer segment
- Evidence type (official documentation, customer statement, independent research, media, etc.)
- Confidence and limitations
- Whether it is a fact, interpretation, assumption, or hypothesis

Do not present a vendor's marketing claim as independent proof of customer outcomes.

## Opportunity pathways

Evaluate these before product development:
1. Partner with an established provider.
2. Distribute or resell an existing solution.
3. Implement, configure, train, or support customers using it.
4. Integrate existing systems or data sources.
5. Supply a complementary service or intelligence capability.
6. Build only after validating a genuine gap and concrete incentive.

## Decision outcomes

- **Continue research:** insufficient evidence.
- **Partner / distribute:** an existing solution addresses the need.
- **Implement / integrate:** adoption or interoperability is the main problem.
- **Complement:** a verified missing capability can be supplied independently.
- **Monitor:** a market gap exists, but another provider may be addressing it or timing is premature.
- **Build consideration:** only after customer need, differentiation, feasibility, and incentive are substantiated.

A gap alone is not a go-ahead.
