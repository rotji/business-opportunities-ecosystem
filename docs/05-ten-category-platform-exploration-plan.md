# Ten-Category Platform Exploration Plan

**Status:** Exploration plan — categories established; platform selections and commercial conclusions remain open.

## 1. Purpose

We will explore the business technology and intelligence ecosystem through a deliberately broad but manageable set of categories. Rather than studying many similar platforms in one category at the same time, we will select **one representative platform per category** and investigate it properly.

The objective is to understand how different parts of the business ecosystem work, which companies serve them, how those companies make money, and whether there are practical ways for us to participate commercially.

This is a market investigation, not a commitment to build software or become a reseller of every platform we examine.

## 2. The ten categories

| No. | Category | Scope of investigation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business Management Systems | Systems businesses use to manage internal operations: ERP, CRM, HRM, POS, accounting, inventory, procurement, workflows and related functions. |
| 2 | Business Intelligence | Tools that analyse business data and turn it into reports, dashboards, performance measures, forecasts and decision support. |
| 3 | External Business Intelligence | Tools and services that help businesses understand their external environment: competitors, markets, suppliers, regulation, industry developments, geography and economic signals. |
| 4 | Data Infrastructure | Platforms and services for collecting, storing, processing, integrating, governing, enriching, verifying and exchanging data; includes APIs and data marketplaces. |
| 5 | Automation and AI | Workflow automation, AI assistants and agents, intelligent document processing, orchestration and automation of business tasks. |
| 6 | Payments and Financial Infrastructure | Payment acceptance, transaction processing, financial APIs and other infrastructure that enables businesses to move or manage money. |
| 7 | Logistics and Supply Chain Infrastructure | Delivery, fulfilment, warehousing, distribution, shipment visibility and supply-chain coordination. |
| 8 | Cloud and Computing Infrastructure | Hosting, cloud computing, storage, databases, developer infrastructure and related managed services. |
| 9 | Communication and Customer Engagement | Business messaging, contact-centre tools, customer support, email/SMS/voice infrastructure and customer engagement platforms. |
| 10 | Identity, Security and Trust Infrastructure | Authentication, access management, cybersecurity, identity verification, fraud prevention and trust-related services. |

**Classification note:** These are practical exploration categories, not mutually exclusive industry boundaries. A company may operate across several categories. We will classify the platform according to the main capability we are studying and note important overlaps.

Business Intelligence (Category 2) and External Business Intelligence (Category 3) are intentionally separate: the first focuses mainly on analysing business information and performance; the second focuses on information about the world outside the business.

## 3. Platform selection rule: one representative platform at a time

For each category:

1. Identify a suitable candidate platform.
2. Evaluate it against the selection criteria below.
3. If it is suitable, make it the active platform for that category and investigate it in depth.
4. If it is unsuitable, document why and select an alternative.
5. Do not automatically study five or ten similar competitors. Expand to alternatives only when evidence, comparison, or a specific unanswered question makes that necessary.

A candidate is not a final selection merely because it appears in a list or has been mentioned in conversation. Selection follows practical inspection.

## 4. Access and exploration preference

We are at an early exploration stage and do not want short trial periods to dictate the pace of research.

Prioritise platforms that offer one or more of the following:

- a permanent free plan;
- recurring free usage, tokens or credits;
- a free tier with enough functionality to understand the product;
- a public demo, sandbox or interactive product tour that does not require a time-limited trial;
- useful public documentation, videos, case studies, webinars or other materials that allow meaningful investigation without activating a trial.

A seven-day or similarly short free trial is **not a preferred starting point**. Keep such a platform on the candidate list if it is strategically important, but do not activate the trial merely to browse. We may reconsider it later when there is a specific, time-bounded reason to test it.

Free access is a research-access preference, not proof that the platform is commercially suitable, reliable, available in Nigeria, or appropriate for customers.

## 5. Platform evaluation criteria

Assess each candidate using these questions:

### Product and customer
- What does the platform actually do?
- Which business problem does it solve?
- Who is the intended customer and user?
- What types and sizes of organisations use it?
- What evidence exists of real customer adoption?

### Commercial model
- What are its plans, pricing structure and usage limits?
- Who pays: individual users, teams, businesses, developers, partners or another group?
- What appears to drive revenue: subscriptions, usage, transactions, licensing, services or a combination?
- What is included in free access, and what requires payment?

### Market and distribution
- How does the company attract customers?
- What channels does it use: content, search, advertising, webinars, events, outbound sales, communities or partners?
- Which markets and geographies does it serve?
- Are there meaningful barriers for Nigerian customers, such as payment, currency, compliance, connectivity, support or availability?

### Ecosystem and participation
- Does it have partner, reseller, referral, affiliate or implementation programmes?
- Does it support consultants, agencies, developers or service providers?
- Are APIs, integrations, marketplaces or certification programmes available?
- What are the eligibility requirements, costs, commercial terms and geographic restrictions?
- Which practical route could be available to us from a zero-network starting point?

### Opportunity
- Could we help businesses discover, select, adopt, configure or use the platform?
- Could we distribute or resell it, if the company permits this?
- Could we provide implementation, training, integration or ongoing support?
- Is there a complementary service or intelligence need?
- What evidence is still missing before we can make a commercial decision?

## 6. Practical investigation process

We will work through one category at a time and one active platform at a time.

1. **Find the official website.** Confirm the company and product identity.
2. **Inspect the public product information.** Understand the homepage, product pages, use cases, documentation, demo and pricing.
3. **Access the product carefully.** Prefer free plans, credits, sandbox access or public demos. Do not start a short trial without a specific reason.
4. **Map the customer and use case.** Identify who buys, who uses it and what job it performs.
5. **Understand the business model.** Examine pricing, packaging, sales approach and likely revenue sources.
6. **Study go-to-market activity.** Look at marketing, customer acquisition, case studies, events, content and distribution.
7. **Investigate commercial access.** Find official partner, reseller, affiliate, developer, integration and contact channels.
8. **Explore practical engagement.** Where justified, contact the company through its official website, email, phone or professional channels. Begin remotely; use in-person engagement only when useful or necessary.
9. **Assess our possible role.** Identify realistic ways to participate and what qualifications, resources or proof would be required.
10. **Agree on findings before documenting conclusions.** Discussion and practical understanding come first. GitHub records should reflect discoveries we have actually examined and agreed upon, not speculative lists presented as completed research.

## 7. Practical constraints and operating assumptions

- We are starting without established personal connections or a partner network.
- Initial engagement should therefore be possible remotely through websites, email, phone and official application channels.
- We should not assume a programme accepts applicants from Nigeria; verify geographic eligibility directly.
- We should not assume that a free plan includes commercial use, partner rights or customer resale rights.
- Do not pay for subscriptions, activate short trials, submit sensitive information or make commercial commitments just to continue exploration.
- A provider's marketing claims must be distinguished from independently verified evidence and our own observations.

## 8. How we will record each investigation

Once findings have been explored and agreed, the relevant category record should capture:

- category and investigation question;
- selected platform and official URLs;
- why it was selected, including access conditions;
- product capabilities and target customers;
- pricing and free-tier limits, with date checked;
- business model and go-to-market observations;
- partner and integration routes;
- geographic availability and barriers;
- practical contacts or next engagement actions;
- evidence, sources, dates and confidence;
- our interpretation, hypotheses and unresolved questions;
- decision: continue, replace candidate, engage, monitor or close investigation;
- exact next step.

Keep evidence, findings, interpretation, hypotheses, decisions and unknowns separate.

## 9. Order of exploration

The initial sequence is:

1. Business Management Systems
2. Business Intelligence
3. External Business Intelligence
4. Data Infrastructure
5. Automation and AI
6. Payments and Financial Infrastructure
7. Logistics and Supply Chain Infrastructure
8. Cloud and Computing Infrastructure
9. Communication and Customer Engagement
10. Identity, Security and Trust Infrastructure

The sequence is a starting order, not a promise that every category will receive equal time. We may adjust it when practical discoveries justify a change, while preserving the reason for doing so.

## 10. What success looks like

The outcome is not simply a list of ten software products. For each category, we want to understand:

- the market and customer problems;
- at least one real provider and its actual solution;
- how the provider earns revenue and reaches customers;
- the surrounding partner and integration ecosystem;
- the practical barriers and opportunities in Nigeria;
- whether there is a credible way for us to create value without building a competing product.

Across the ten categories, this should give us a broader picture of the business technology ecosystem and help identify where a realistic commercial opportunity exists.

## 11. Governance

This plan is governed by [Project Governance](00-project-governance.md) and the project's [market-first, build-last philosophy](01-governing-philosophy.md).

We must check existing repository records before each new investigation, preserve discoveries, avoid repeating completed work, and revisit earlier conclusions only when new evidence or a justified change in scope warrants it.

**Current state:** The ten categories and investigation method are agreed as the working exploration plan. No platform has yet been formally selected for all ten categories. Category 1—Business Management Systems—is the intended first practical investigation.
